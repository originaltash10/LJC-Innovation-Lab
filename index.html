Method
ProcessMethod

Six steps, one prototype

The lab takes a single architectural component the whole way: from AI-assisted exploration to a physical object people can touch. Select a step to read it.

Goal

Build a prototype, not a rendering.

  1. Step 01
    Explore and refine

    Choose one architectural component and push it with AI-assisted design and workflows.

  2. Step 02
    Make it printable

    Translate the concept into fabrication-ready 3D geometry: panelised, toleranced, toolpath-aware.

  3. Step 03
    Print small, in house

    Test and improve the component through iterative small-scale 3D-printed mockups on our own printer.

  4. Step 04
    Bring in a manufacturer

    Evaluate materials, scalability and fabrication feasibility with a partner, with cost in the room from day one.

  5. Step 05
    Make it repeatable

    Establish a workflow that connects design exploration to physical production, so the next component is faster.

  6. Step 06
    Install at 1:1

    Print and install a full-scale prototype in the RBB lobby, where the whole office walks past it.

Why the OperaTesting ground

A theater is the right test

Opera is built on story, material and spectacle. Conceptual studies in form for the printed wall, each drawn from a different part of the house. Select one, or let it cycle.

01 · Opera curtain
Reference

The house curtain — fold, weight, and the pause before the story starts.

Exposition, climax, resolution — one stage, three states. The wall reads the same way as you climb.

Where the voice hits the wall, the wall responds. Every ridge is a continuous toolpath.

Location analysisDecision 1

Three prime locations

We analyzed the current plans for a wall that is tall, seen by everyone, and shaped in a way that helps the concept. Pick a location on the plan.

C · Main staircase Recommended

Main entrance, exterior

A two-storey curved facade under the canopy. The most public of the three, and the most exposed.

  • Street-facing: first thing a visitor sees
  • Exterior exposure adds material, UV and weathering constraints
  • Height is capped by the canopy

Bar wall

A curved two-level wall facing the lounge and the glazing beyond.

  • Intimate, close-range viewing while people wait
  • Partly covered by bar fit-out and back-of-bar equipment
  • Shorter wall, read mostly from one side

Main staircase

The wall that wraps the feature stair from Level 1 to Level 3.

  • Runs the full height of the space
  • Highly visible from the lobby, the stair and both landings
  • The curve at the intermediate landing gives the concept a natural turn
  • Seen up close and at a distance, moving past it
Conceptual studies in formDecision 2

Three ways to interpret the opera

Each concept is a different source for the geometry. All three are generated parametrically, so panel size, depth and density stay open until we print.

The curtain, frozen

The gathered folds of a house curtain, held still in print. Soft drape reads from across the lobby; the ridges land at hand height.

Three studies, from literal swag to a pure striated field. The middle one is the strongest in the stair — it reads as fabric without copying one.

Three acts in relief

Exposition, conflict, catharsis. Calm layer lines at the base build into turbulence at the landing and resolve as you reach the top.

The arc maps naturally onto the stair: you climb through the story. Hover the panels to trace it.

Sound made solid

The amplitude, movement and bounce of sound in the room, mapped into surface depth. Where the voice would hit the wall, the wall responds.

The most abstract of the three, and the one that most directly belongs to an opera house. It also prints cleanly: every ridge is a continuous toolpath.

Road to 1:1Four stages

From desk print to lobby

A proposed sequence, dates to be set. Each stage ends with something physical on the table.

Stage 01
Down-select, in house

Small-scale prints of the two shortlisted concepts on the lab printer. Pick one.

Stage 02
Partner mockup

One bay of the wall printed by the chosen fabricator. Material, seams, finish and cost confirmed.

Stage 03
Full-scale print

1:1 prototype printed and finished. Workflow documented so the next component is faster.

Stage 04
Install

Prototype installed in the RBB lobby. Feedback from the office feeds the Opera design.

Destination

The RBB lobby — tall, daylit, and walked through by everyone. The prototype lives where the firm can react to it.

The askToday

Four decisions, one wall

Everything else the lab can carry. These four we need from you.

  1. Confirm the stair wall

    Lock the main staircase as the site for the printed element so we design to its true height and curve.

  2. Pick one concept

    Choose which of curtain, dramatic arc and sound waves goes forward to print.

  3. Approve a quote request

    Let us ask one or two fabricators for a single-bay mockup price and lead time.

  4. Reserve a location in the lobby

    Hold a spot in the RBB lobby for the 1:1 prototype — it may be free-standing rather than wall-mounted.
